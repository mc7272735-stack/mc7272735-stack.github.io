The exceptional set of Goldbach numbers among polynomial values

with Ingo Althöfer, Alberto Perelli and János Pintz

Keywords: Goldbach exceptional sets · Circle method · Polynomial exponential sums · Mean-value estimates

Abstract

Let φ\phi be a fixed polynomial with integer coefficients, positive leading coefficient and degree k≥2k\geq2. We prove that the values 2φ(n)2\phi(n), 1≤n≤N1\leq n\leq N, can be written as the sum of two primes, apart from at most Nαk+o(1)N^{\alpha_k+o(1)} exceptional indices, where αk<1\alpha_k<1. For quadratic polynomials the exponent is 121/2, for cubic polynomials 585/8, for quartic polynomials 343/4, and for k>4k>4 it is 1−1k+11-1/(k+1). The proof combines a fourth-moment estimate over primes with mean-value estimates over arbitrary subsets of polynomial sequences.

A Counterexample to Wooley’s L¹ Question for Beatty Sequences

Keywords: Beatty sequences · Discrete Fourier analysis · Diophantine approximation · Continued fractions

Abstract

For real numbers α\alpha and β\beta, let B(α,β)={⌊αm+β⌋:m∈N}\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\beta)=\{\lfloor\alpha m+\beta\rfloor:m\in\mathbb{N}\}. Wooley asked whether the exponential sum over B(α,β)∩[1,N]\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\beta)\cap[1,N] always has L1L^1 norm Oα,β(logN)O_{\alpha,\beta}(\log N). We give a negative answer. We construct fixed α>1\alpha>1 and β\beta, with 1α1/\alpha of finite Diophantine type, such that the L1L^1 norm is ≫(logN)2\gg(\log N)^2 for infinitely many NN. Wooley also proved that this L1L^1 norm is Oα,β((log(2N))2)O_{\alpha,\beta}((\log(2N))^2) when 1α1/\alpha has finite Diophantine type. Our example shows that his upper bound is sharp.

Hecke Eigenform Product Identities for Full-Level Hilbert Modular Forms

with Bowen Gan, Chao Qin and Yang Zhou

Keywords: Hilbert modular forms · Hecke eigenforms · Rankin–Selberg method · Special values of L-functions

Abstract

We study full-level Hilbert Hecke eigenform product identities over totally real fields of degree at least two, allowing arbitrary compatible narrow class characters. Combining our results with previous work, we classify products of two Eisenstein eigenforms of positive parallel integral weights and products of an Eisenstein eigenform of weight at least two with a cuspidal eigenform of positive parallel integral weight. The unique identity in each of these two classes is given by E4=60E22E_{4}=60 E_{2}^{2} and h8=120E2·h6h_{8}=120 E_{2} \cdot h_{6}, respectively, both over Q(5)\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}). The proofs use constant-term comparisons, indecomposable Fourier exponents, integrality of reciprocal Eisenstein constant terms, and a Rankin-Selberg formula for Petersson inner products. In the remaining case of a weight-one Eisenstein factor and a cuspidal factor, we also establish h5=12E1(1,ε)·h4h_5=12E_1(1,\varepsilon)\cdot h_4 over Q(3)\mathbb Q(\sqrt3), where ε\varepsilon is the nontrivial narrow class character. All eigenforms in these identities have unit-ideal coefficient one.

Rigidity of Averages over the Two Largest Prime Factors

Keywords: Prime-factor distributions · Dickman kernels · Convolution methods · Wiener’s Tauberian theorem

Abstract

Let P1(n)P_1(n) and P2(n)P_2(n) be the largest and second-largest distinct prime factors of nn. Alladi and Johnson asked whether there is a bounded function ff on the primes such that the averages 1x∑2≤n≤xf(P1(n))\frac{1}{x}\sum_{2\le n\le x}f(P_1(n)) and 1x∑2≤n≤xf(P2(n))\frac{1}{x}\sum_{2\le n\le x}f(P_2(n)) converge to different limits, where f(P2(n))=0f(P_2(n))=0 when nn is a prime power. We show that this cannot happen. In fact, whenever the first average converges, the second also converges to the same limit.

Preprint · 2026

Bounded Ratios of Lorentzian Polynomials I: The Ternary Theory and Optimal Bounding Constants

with Bowen Gan, Ivy Liu, Zemeng Wang and Chengzhi Wu

Keywords: Lorentzian polynomials · Algebraic combinatorics · Bounded-ratio cones · Variational formulas · Mixed volumes

Abstract

We study bounded ratios and optimal bounding constants among the normalized coefficients of ternary Lorentzian polynomials. For every fixed MM-convex support and in arbitrary degree, we give an explicit presentation of the bounded-ratio cone in terms of quadratic Hessian slices. We then express the optimal bounding constants through a variational formula combining local support functions with linear compatibility constraints between slices. For full support, we determine all compatibility relations in arbitrary degree; in degree three, this yields explicit optimal constants for every two-generator section. Finally, we compare the resulting Lorentzian bounds with those for volume polynomials and rank-three matroid basis profiles.

Bounded Ratios of Lorentzian Polynomials II: The Complete Quadratic Local-to-Global Classification

with Bowen Gan, Ivy Liu, Zemeng Wang and Chengzhi Wu

Keywords: Lorentzian polynomials · Algebraic combinatorics · Local-to-global principles · M-convexity · Exponential degenerations

Abstract

Every quadratic Hessian slice of a Lorentzian polynomial yields bounded monomial ratios among the normalized coefficients of the polynomial. We determine exactly for which pairs (n,d)(n,d) these quadratic-slice ratios generate the full bounded-ratio cone for every MM-convex support S⊆ΔndS\subseteq\Delta_n^d. For d≥2d\geq 2, this quadratic local-to-global principle holds universally if and only if n≤3,d=2,or(n,d)=(4,3)n\leq 3,\qquad d=2,\qquad\text{or}\qquad(n,d)=(4,3). In every remaining case, the principle fails already for Lorentzian polynomials with full support: for cubics in n≥5n\geq5 variables and for polynomials of degree d≥4d\geq4 in n≥4n\geq4 variables. We identify the two minimal obstructions, at (n,d)=(4,4)(n,d)=(4,4) and (n,d)=(5,3)(n,d)=(5,3), and propagate them throughout the failure region by degree and variable aggregation.
