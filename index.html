Research Interests

My research interests are rooted in analysis and number theory, particularly complex analysis, harmonic analysis, and analytic number theory.

Broader interests

Number Theory

My interests in number theory center on analytic and probabilistic number theory and arithmetic statistics. I am particularly drawn to additive and multiplicative problems involving exponential sums, the circle method, and the distribution of primes and prime factors. Other interests include the statistical behavior of class groups and Selmer groups of elliptic curves, as well as analytic aspects of automorphic forms and L-functions. These interests also lead me to connections with harmonic analysis, combinatorics, and ergodic theory, especially in the study of multiplicative functions and polynomial patterns.

Complex Analysis

My interests in complex analysis include geometric function theory and several complex variables. In several complex variables, I am particularly interested in the local geometry and holomorphic equivalence of real submanifolds with CR singularities. I am also drawn to the analytic theory of the ∂̄-equation, including integral representation formulas, homotopy operators, L2 estimates, and regularity theory.

Recent interests

Recently, I have been exploring the following directions:

  • Harmonic analysis and arithmetic: higher-order Fourier analysis, additive combinatorics, and decoupling, with connections to multiplicative functions, polynomial patterns, and exponential sums.
  • Automorphic representations and L-functions: representations of general linear groups and their associated L-functions, analytic properties of Artin L-functions, and applications of large sieve methods to the study of L-functions.
  • Several complex variables and CR geometry: CR singularities, holomorphic dynamics, and connections to potential theory.

The exceptional set of Goldbach numbers among polynomial values

with Ingo Althöfer, Alberto Perelli and János Pintz

Keywords: Goldbach exceptional sets · Circle method · Polynomial exponential sums · Mean-value estimates

Abstract

Let φ\phi be a fixed polynomial with integer coefficients, positive leading coefficient and degree k≥2k\geq2. We prove that the values 2φ(n)2\phi(n), 1≤n≤N1\leq n\leq N, can be written as the sum of two primes, apart from at most Nαk+o(1)N^{\alpha_k+o(1)} exceptional indices, where αk<1\alpha_k<1. For quadratic polynomials the exponent is 121/2, for cubic polynomials 585/8, for quartic polynomials 343/4, and for k>4k>4 it is 1−1k+11-1/(k+1). The proof combines a fourth-moment estimate over primes with mean-value estimates over arbitrary subsets of polynomial sequences.

A Counterexample to Wooley’s L¹ Question for Beatty Sequences

Keywords: Beatty sequences · Discrete Fourier analysis · Diophantine approximation · Continued fractions

Abstract

For real numbers α\alpha and β\beta, let B(α,β)={⌊αm+β⌋:m∈N}\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\beta)=\{\lfloor\alpha m+\beta\rfloor:m\in\mathbb{N}\}. Wooley asked whether the exponential sum over B(α,β)∩[1,N]\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\beta)\cap[1,N] always has L1L^1 norm Oα,β(logN)O_{\alpha,\beta}(\log N). We give a negative answer. We construct fixed α>1\alpha>1 and β\beta, with 1α1/\alpha of finite Diophantine type, such that the L1L^1 norm is ≫(logN)2\gg(\log N)^2 for infinitely many NN. Wooley also proved that this L1L^1 norm is Oα,β((log(2N))2)O_{\alpha,\beta}((\log(2N))^2) when 1α1/\alpha has finite Diophantine type. Our example shows that his upper bound is sharp.

Hecke Eigenform Product Identities for Full-Level Hilbert Modular Forms

with Bowen Gan, Chao Qin and Yang Zhou

Keywords: Hilbert modular forms · Hecke eigenforms · Rankin–Selberg method · Special values of L-functions

Abstract

We study full-level Hilbert Hecke eigenform product identities over totally real fields of degree at least two, allowing arbitrary compatible narrow class characters. Combining our results with previous work, we classify products of two Eisenstein eigenforms of positive parallel integral weights and products of an Eisenstein eigenform of weight at least two with a cuspidal eigenform of positive parallel integral weight. The unique identity in each of these two classes is given by E4=60E22E_{4}=60 E_{2}^{2} and h8=120E2·h6h_{8}=120 E_{2} \cdot h_{6}, respectively, both over Q(5)\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}). The proofs use constant-term comparisons, indecomposable Fourier exponents, integrality of reciprocal Eisenstein constant terms, and a Rankin-Selberg formula for Petersson inner products. In the remaining case of a weight-one Eisenstein factor and a cuspidal factor, we also establish h5=12E1(1,ε)·h4h_5=12E_1(1,\varepsilon)\cdot h_4 over Q(3)\mathbb Q(\sqrt3), where ε\varepsilon is the nontrivial narrow class character. All eigenforms in these identities have unit-ideal coefficient one.

Rigidity of Averages over the Two Largest Prime Factors

Keywords: Prime-factor distributions · Dickman kernels · Convolution methods · Wiener’s Tauberian theorem

Abstract

Let P1(n)P_1(n) and P2(n)P_2(n) be the largest and second-largest distinct prime factors of nn. Alladi and Johnson asked whether there is a bounded function ff on the primes such that the averages 1x∑2≤n≤xf(P1(n))\frac{1}{x}\sum_{2\le n\le x}f(P_1(n)) and 1x∑2≤n≤xf(P2(n))\frac{1}{x}\sum_{2\le n\le x}f(P_2(n)) converge to different limits, where f(P2(n))=0f(P_2(n))=0 when nn is a prime power. We show that this cannot happen. In fact, whenever the first average converges, the second also converges to the same limit.

Sharpness of the Brjuno Condition for Hyperbolic Bishop Surfaces

Keywords: Hyperbolic Bishop surfaces · CR singularities · Brjuno condition · Holomorphic linearization · Potential theory

Abstract

Let μ ∈ ℂ satisfy |μ| = 1, and assume that μ is not a root of unity and is not Brjuno. Let Qγ denote the Bishop quadric with γ=12+μ+μ−1>12. Gong and Stolovitch conjectured that there exists a real-analytic surface in ℂ2 with Moser–Webster invariant μ that is formally but not holomorphically equivalent to Qγ. We prove this conjecture.

Preprint · 2026

Bounded Ratios of Lorentzian Polynomials I: The Ternary Theory and Optimal Bounding Constants

with Bowen Gan, Ivy Liu, Zemeng Wang and Chengzhi Wu

Keywords: Lorentzian polynomials · Algebraic combinatorics · Bounded-ratio cones · Variational formulas · Mixed volumes

Abstract

We study bounded coefficient ratios and their optimal bounding constants for ternary Lorentzian polynomials. For every degree d ≥ 2 and every fixed ternary M-convex support, we prove that bounded ratios are generated by those of quadratic derivatives. We express the optimal bounding constants through a variational formula. For full support, we determine all linear relations among the logarithms of triangular ratios in every degree. For ternary cubics, we compute sharp constants for products of any two distinct triangular ratios. Finally, we compare the bounds for Lorentzian polynomials with those for volume polynomials and rank-three matroid basis counts.

Preprint · 2026

Bounded Ratios of Lorentzian Polynomials II: Quadratic Derivatives

with Bowen Gan, Ivy Liu, Zemeng Wang and Chengzhi Wu

Keywords: Lorentzian polynomials · Algebraic combinatorics · Bounded ratios · Quadratic derivatives · Discrete concavity · M-convexity

Abstract

We study bounded monomial ratios for Lorentzian polynomials. We prove that, in degree d ≥ 2 and n variables, the bounded ratios from quadratic derivatives generate all bounded ratios for every M-convex support if and only if n ≤ 3, d = 2, or (n, d) = (4, 3). For four-variable cubics, the proof uses a decomposition into M-concave functions. We construct explicit counterexamples with full support for four-variable quartics and five-variable cubics, then extend these two examples to all remaining pairs.

October 8, 2026 Number Theory Seminar, University of Wisconsin–Madison

Rigidity of Averages over the Two Largest Prime Factors

I discuss my resolution of a question of Alladi and Johnson concerning averages over the two largest distinct prime factors of integers. For a bounded function on the primes, convergence of the average over the largest prime factor forces the average over the second-largest distinct prime factor to converge to the same limit. The proof combines smooth-number estimates, Dickman-type convolution kernels, and Wiener’s Tauberian theorem.

Paper: D. Chen, Rigidity of Averages over the Two Largest Prime Factors, arXiv:2608.05191.

Expository note · 5 pages

The Quadratic Lorentzian Cone: A Self-Contained Proof of the Closure Property

An independently developed proof using Perron–Frobenius theory, explicit perturbations, and Schur complements, written during a reading group on Brändén and Huh’s Lorentzian Polynomials under the guidance of Botong Wang.

A shorter proof — from a private conversation with Petter Brändén

For a nonzero entrywise nonnegative symmetric matrix A with at most one positive eigenvalue, let J be the all-ones matrix and set A(s) = (I + sJ)A(I + sJ), s > 0. This invertible congruence preserves inertia by Sylvester’s law, while making every entry strictly positive; moreover, A(s) tends to A as s tends to zero. The zero matrix is approximated separately by εJ − ε2I. Brändén also explained the connection with the proof of Theorem 2.13.
