Research Talks

October 8, 2026 Number Theory Seminar, University of Wisconsin–Madison

Rigidity of Averages over the Two Largest Prime Factors

I discuss my resolution of a question of Alladi and Johnson concerning averages over the two largest distinct prime factors of integers. For a bounded function on the primes, convergence of the average over the largest prime factor forces the average over the second-largest distinct prime factor to converge to the same limit. The proof combines smooth-number estimates, Dickman-type convolution kernels, and Wiener’s Tauberian theorem.

Paper: D. Chen, Rigidity of Averages over the Two Largest Prime Factors, arXiv:2608.05191.

Expository Talks & Reading Seminars

November 6, 2026 Graduate Analysis Seminar (Math 921), University of Wisconsin–Madison

The Sum–Product Conjecture Is False for Real Numbers

An exposition of a counterexample to the Erdős–Szemerédi sum–product conjecture over the real numbers. I focus on the algebraic number theory behind the construction, especially how the additive lattice of algebraic integers and the multiplicative lattice of units can be combined to produce sets with simultaneously small sumsets and product sets.

Reference: T. F. Bloom, W. Sawin, C. Schildkraut, and D. Zhelezov, The sum-product conjecture is false for real numbers, arXiv:2605.28781 (2026).

April 24, 2026 Graduate Analysis Seminar (Math 921), University of Wisconsin–Madison

Quantitative Nondivergence and Its Diophantine Applications

An exposition of quantitative nondivergence estimates for trajectories in the space of unimodular lattices and their applications to metric Diophantine approximation. The talk emphasizes connections between homogeneous dynamics, lattice geometry, and number theory.

Reference: D. Kleinbock, Quantitative Nondivergence and its Diophantine Applications, in Homogeneous Flows, Moduli Spaces and Arithmetic, Clay Mathematics Proceedings 10 (2010), 131–153.

March 5, 2026 Lorentzian Polynomials Directed Study Group, University of Wisconsin–Madison

Lorentzian Polynomials — Part III: Completion of Section 2.1

Directed study under the supervision of Professor Botong Wang.

I completed the presentation of Section 2.1 and the proof of Theorem 2.13 on approximation by strictly Lorentzian polynomials. In this shared session, Ivy Liu began the subsequent work toward closedness of the Lorentzian class.

Reference: P. Brändén and J. Huh, Lorentzian polynomials, Annals of Mathematics 192 (2020), 821–891.

Lorentzian Polynomials — Part II: Structural Properties and Approximation

Directed study under the supervision of Professor Botong Wang.

November 18, 2025 Graduate Number Theory Seminar, University of Wisconsin–Madison

Roth’s Theorem on Three-Term Arithmetic Progressions: The Original Proof

A presentation of Roth’s original proof that every subset of the positive integers with positive upper density contains a nontrivial three-term arithmetic progression. I focus on reconstructing the original ideas and arguments.

Reference: K. F. Roth, On Certain Sets of Integers, Journal of the London Mathematical Society 28 (1953), 104–109.

October 31, 2025 Graduate Analysis Seminar (Math 921), University of Wisconsin–Madison

Vinogradov’s Method

An introduction to Vinogradov’s method for estimating exponential sums, focusing on the main ideas and an alternative derivation of a key estimate.

Reference: H. L. Montgomery, Ten Lectures on the Interface between Analytic Number Theory and Harmonic Analysis, CBMS Regional Conference Series in Mathematics 84 (1994), Chapter 4, Sections 4.1 and 4.4.
