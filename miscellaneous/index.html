Expository note · 5 pages

The Quadratic Lorentzian Cone: A Self-Contained Proof of the Closure Property

An independently developed proof using Perron–Frobenius theory, explicit perturbations, and Schur complements, written during a reading group on Brändén and Huh’s Lorentzian Polynomials under the guidance of Botong Wang.

A shorter proof — from a private conversation with Petter Brändén

For a nonzero entrywise nonnegative symmetric matrix A with at most one positive eigenvalue, let J be the all-ones matrix and set A(s) = (I + sJ)A(I + sJ), s > 0. This invertible congruence preserves inertia by Sylvester’s law, while making every entry strictly positive; moreover, A(s) tends to A as s tends to zero. The zero matrix is approximated separately by εJ − ε2I. Brändén also explained the connection with the proof of Theorem 2.13.

The Small-Sphere Residue Constant for Webster’s Bochner–Martinelli–Koppelman Kernel

This expository note provides a self-contained treatment of the small-sphere residue constant for the Bochner–Martinelli–Koppelman kernel in Webster's notation, together with a derivation of the classical homotopy formula. Particular attention is paid to orientations, signs, and normalization.

I am grateful to Professor Xianghong Gong for suggesting this problem and for providing helpful references.

Reading notes · First drafted in December 2025 · 92 pages

Sarnak’s Conjecture and Möbius Disjointness: Results, Methods, and Detailed Proofs

These working notes collect fragmentary reflections on Möbius disjointness and related results, together with reading strategies for the original papers. They preserve my thought process as I explore how different methods address the lack of correlation between arithmetic sequences and low-complexity dynamical systems.

A note to the reader

Scattered throughout are questions and tentative ideas that arose during my own reading and conversations with AI and friends. Some struck me as worth pursuing; others may simply reflect a beginner’s confusion. I have kept them as possible companions to the reader’s own thinking—please feel free to follow the interesting detours and skip the rest.

First drafted early in my study of the subject, these notes may contain omissions, misunderstandings, and errors, for which I take full responsibility. Please use them with care and consult the original sources.
